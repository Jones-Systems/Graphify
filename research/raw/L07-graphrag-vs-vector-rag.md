## Where each approach wins (dated synthesis)

Pure vector / hybrid-lexical retrieval wins:
- Single-hop factoid lookup over docs: NQ F1 64.8 (vector) vs 63.0 (best graph variant), identical generator (arXiv 2502.11371, Feb 2025).
- Shallow, lexically-explicit two-hop questions: hybrid BM25+dense with reranking remains competitive-to-better and far simpler (arXiv 2407.01219, Jul 2024; corroborated by routing heuristics in 2502.11371 discussion).
- Cost/latency-critical paths: no index-time LLM tax; chunking hygiene alone moves recall ~9 pts (Chroma, Jul 2024).
- Basic factual recall after graph augmentation is NOT automatically safe: naive graph-augmented RAG regressed below plain RAG on factual tasks until HippoRAG 2 fixed it (arXiv 2502.14802, Feb 2025) - the strongest caution against replacing vector retrieval.

KG / graph-based retrieval wins:
- Cross-document multi-hop where evidence chains are weakly lexicalized: MultiHop-RAG 70.3 vs 67.0 accuracy (eval of Feb 2025); up to +20 percent retrieval over SOTA at 10-30x lower cost than iterative retrieval (HippoRAG, May 2024); +26.5 percent over CoT with KG-grounded stepwise traversal (Feb 2025).
- Corpus-global sensemaking / query-focused summarization (main themes across the whole corpus): MS GraphRAG community summaries substantially beat vector RAG on comprehensiveness and diversity (Apr 2024).
- Long-document holistic QA through hierarchy rather than entities: RAPTOR +20 absolute on QuALITY, gains on NarrativeQA/QASPER (Jan 2024).
- Evolving temporal enterprise/conversational memory: Zep +18.5 LongMemEval accuracy, -90 percent latency vs baselines (Jan 2025).
- Hardest domain-specific textbook reasoning where simple retrieval provably fails: GraphRAG-Bench quantifies graph-structuring benefits across 16 disciplines (Jun 2025).
- Financial/transcript-style documentation: fusion of KG + vectors beats each individually at both retrieval and generation stages (HybridRAG, Aug 2024).

Cross-source consensus caveats: graph gains shrink to ~2-3 pts or vanish for well-localized lookups; entity/triple extraction errors propagate into retrieval quality; index-time LLM cost is the dominant practical barrier (MS explicitly warns GraphRAG indexing is expensive); stepwise traversal control matters more than graph size; and all strong graph systems still keep dense/vector passage scores in the loop.

## Verdict

Top pick: HippoRAG-style hybrid on top of assets we already own - BM25 lexical seeds + Personalized PageRank over the existing graphifyy structural graph (markdown link-graph + code AST), passages kept first-class, with a trivial router that sends single-hop queries straight to lexical/hybrid search; adopt LightRAG (MIT, lightrag-hku 1.5.6, official offline guide, local Qwen3-class support) as the LLM-KG vehicle once a CPU-viable local LLM is approved.
Why: every rigorous comparison found (2502.11371 Feb 2025, GraphRAG-Bench Jun 2025, HippoRAG 1/2 2024-2025, RAPTOR Jan 2024, HybridRAG Aug 2024) agrees graphs pay off specifically on cross-doc multi-hop and corpus-global queries while vector/hybrid stays equal-or-better elsewhere, and the largest reliable wins come from FUSING graph signals with lexical/vector scores rather than replacing them.
Integration sketch: phase 1 (zero LLM): persistent chunk store + BM25 + scipy/networkx PPR over current NetworkX JSON, evaluated against MultiHop-RAG-style held-out multi-hop queries plus a single-hop set to guard regression; phase 2 (requires-owner-approval): llama.cpp/vLLM serving a Qwen3-30B-A3B-class MoE locally, feeding LightRAG or HippoRAG 2 extraction over the same chunks; any cloud-API path carries the requires-owner-approval flag permanently.
