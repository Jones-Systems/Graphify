Integration: mine Codex rollout `event_msg/user_message` texts with Drain3 (mask paths/symbols/numbers) → promote top clusters into (a) literal keyword tier via pickled ahocorasick automaton, (b) paraphrase tier via semantic-router over utterance exemplars with our existing embedder and optimized thresholds, (c) each route maps its extracted slots into a tantivy boolean query; unmatched input abstains into the current BM25+vector+PPR fusion unchanged. Skip Quepy entirely.

## Hit-rate expectations (honest assessment)

**Local corpus reality (sampled ~/.codex/sessions 2026-08-23…25, grounded):** user-turn volume is dominated by harness scaffolding — `<recommended_plugins>` lists, AGENTS.md dumps, approval-review transcripts replayed as user messages — plus machine-templated directives ("Act as a bounded Operational Gate B validator…", "This is a read-only synthetic discovery smoke test…"). Genuine free-form natural-language questions about the codebase are a minority of turns. Two consequences: (1) mining MUST filter to true user prompts and strip angle-bracket blocks before clustering, or templates learn boilerplate; (2) the router's biggest measurable win is correct abstention/dispatch, not answering more queries.

**Expected funnel (coding-agent traffic):** imperative edit/validation directives ≈ majority of prompts → classified non-retrieval, skipped. Of retrieval-shaped prompts: navigation/relation families ("where is X defined", "who calls X", "find usages of Y", "what changed in Z") are regex/keyword-catchable at ~95%+ precision because symbol tokens are unambiguous; estimate literal-tier recall 40–60% of retrieval-shaped prompts ([INFERENCE] from sampled shapes, not measured), rising toward 50–70% once the embedding-exemplar tier covers paraphrase; overall share of ALL prompts answerable via templates stays modest (~10–20%) precisely because most traffic isn't questions. Anything unmatched falls through to the existing tantivy/sqlite-vec/PPR pipeline — the router only adds a cheap exact-answer fast path plus dispatch.

**External calibration:** Affolter, Stockinger & Bernstein, VLDB Journal 28:793–819 (2019) — 24-system comparative survey: keyword systems handle simple lookups only; trigger-word/pattern systems cover basic aggregation but fail nested structure; authors refuse pooled precision/recall because protocols are incomparable; recommend hybrid simple-first/deep-later (exactly our tiering). Spider 2.0 leaderboard snapshot through Apr 2025: best agentic neural text→SQL reaches only ~30–31% execution accuracy (ReFoRCE + o1-preview, ICLR 2025 materials) — even frontier LLMs fail ~70% of hard enterprise NL→SQL, so a no-LLM router promising 40–70% route-coverage *of well-shaped retrieval prompts with abstention* is respectable, not embarrassing. fastText bag-of-tricks (arXiv:1607.01759) shows CPU-trained linear classifiers are viable at our scale; on narrow (<20-label) intent inventories expect >90% P@1 with hundreds of labeled examples ([INFERENCE]; paper's headline numbers are on broad topic sets, e.g. ~64% YahooAnswers).

**Closed loop:** measure per-route family hit rate from the same rollout corpus continuously; unmatched retrieval-shaped prompts become next month's template candidates. Budget: 1 day to stand up mining + literal tier; +0.5 day embedding tier.
